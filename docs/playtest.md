# Playtest protocol

## Purpose

The first human playtests are not primarily balance tests. They test whether the board naturally produces doubt, trust, misdirection, and satisfying reinterpretation at REVEAL.

## Record once per game

- Player missions
- Final score
- CORE HP at end
- MID SUSPICION guesses
- FINAL READ guesses
- Whether each mission was completed

## Count during play

1. Times you wondered whether a move was defense or secret-mission progress.
2. Times you deliberately left a threat for the opponent to handle.
3. Times your current theory of the opponent's mission changed.
4. How difficult it felt to lock MID SUSPICION at Round 4.
5. Times you abandoned mission progress to save the CORE.
6. REVEAL moments that caused a genuine “that explains that move” reaction.

## Qualitative questions

After each game ask both players:

- Which move made you most suspicious?
- Which move did you misread?
- Did you ever act defensively mainly to make your secret action look innocent?
- Did you ever trust the opponent to defend the CORE and regret it?
- Did the opponent's mission explain at least one earlier move after REVEAL?
- Did any mission feel impossible, automatic, or too obvious?

## v0.1.6 watch list

- HUNTER: does 5 defeats feel achievable without becoming completely obvious?
- SHEPHERD: does 4 NUDGEs create useful ambiguity rather than repetitive play?
- PILGRIM: is RUNE movement too easy to read at MID?
- SENTINEL / BAIT: do they genuinely resemble each other during the first half?
- INFILTRATOR: can players reach 3 enemy-territory spaces without abandoning the shared defense?
- BAIT: can a player create the PUSH requirement intentionally without waiting helplessly for the opponent?

## Go / no-go signal

The strongest signal is not win rate. Continue development if most games produce at least one convincing reinterpretation at REVEAL and repeated defense-vs-mission conflicts during the eight rounds.
